ASSUMPTIONS
===========

Assumptions made in building the titre model, and why each was forced.


1. NO BIOREACTOR VOLUME
-----------------------
No volume (V) is supplied in the dataset. Consequently:

  - Dilution terms (F/V) cannot be identified and are omitted from every
    balance.

  - The feed profiles W:FeedGlc and W:FeedGln are treated as additive
    concentration source terms, in the same units as X:Glc and X:Gln per
    day. They are NOT volumetric flows.

  - If V were available, every feed term and every state balance would
    carry a dilution correction of the form:

        dC/dt = ... + (F/V) * (C_in - C)

  - Both Richelle et al. papers (Front. Bioeng. Biotechnol. 10:948905,
    2022; Comput. Struct. Biotechnol. J. 35(1):0078, 2026) carry F/V
    explicitly. We cannot. This is a deliberate simplification forced by
    the data, not an oversight.


2. UNITS OF X:Lysed
-------------------
The challenge ships no data dictionary. X:Lysed is taken to be a
cumulative cell density in the same units as X:VCD, small only because
lysis is slow. Evidence:

  - It is exactly 0.0 at t=0 in all 120 runs: a pool that starts empty,
    not a concentration with an initial condition.

  - It is not a fraction: Xl/Xv is unbounded, and one test row exceeds 1.

  - Over the post-peak decline, VCD falls by a median of 3.04 while
    X:Lysed rises by 0.115, so the lysed pool accounts for roughly 3.4%
    of the viable cells lost. The remainder sits in the dead pool, which
    is not measured.


3. NO VIABILITY OR DEAD-CELL MEASUREMENT
----------------------------------------
The dead-cell pool (Xd) is never observed. It is recovered indirectly
from the lysed-cell trajectory using the sequential lysis form, in which
lysed cells derive from the dead pool:

    dXd/dt = mu_d * Xv - kl * Xd
    dXl/dt = kl * Xd        =>   Xd(t) = (1/kl) * dXl/dt

This makes Xd readable from measured data up to the single constant kl.

Only the RECOVERABILITY of Xd depends on the sequential rather than the
parallel form. The identity that the model actually integrates,

    INT mu_eff * Xv dt = Delta(Xv + Xd + Xl),

follows from either paper's population structure and does not depend on
that choice.

How dXl/dt is obtained is assumption 7.


4. PRODUCT TITRE IS OBSERVED ONLY AT HARVEST
--------------------------------------------
There is one titre value per experiment, at the final observed timepoint.
No titre trajectory exists anywhere in the data. Product formation is
therefore kept to a small number of parameters; a flexible or neural
q_P would not be identifiable against 100 scalar observations.

Product degradation is not modelled, for the same reason.


5. CONTROLLED VARIABLES ARE INPUTS, NOT STATES
----------------------------------------------
Temperature and pH are exact step functions of the Z: scalars, verified
to machine precision across all 1290 rows of both data files:

    W:temp(t)    = tempStart    if t <  tempShift  else tempEnd
    W:pH(t)      = phStart      if t <  phShift    else phEnd
    W:FeedGlc(t) = FeedRateGlc  if FeedStart <= t < FeedEnd  else 0
    W:FeedGln(t) = FeedRateGln  if FeedStart <= t < FeedEnd  else 0

Stirring and dissolved oxygen are per-run constants with no time series.

None of these are given differential equations. Dissolved oxygen is a
controlled setpoint that is handed to us, so the chain from agitation
through kLa to oxygen uptake is deliberately not modelled: it would add
parameters with no oxygen measurement, no OUR and no off-gas data to
constrain them.


6. INFERENCE IS CONDITIONED ON OBSERVED TRAJECTORIES
----------------------------------------------------
The task is to predict final titre, and the /predict payload supplies the
full observed X: trajectories. The model therefore integrates product
formation along measured trajectories rather than simulating them.

Consequence: no differential equation is solved at inference time, and
the viable-cell curve is never numerically differentiated. The only
derivative anywhere is dXl/dt, and that is taken analytically from a
fitted curve rather than by differencing (assumption 7).

Limitation: this model cannot predict an experiment that has not been
run. Forward simulation from design parameters and initial conditions
alone is a different and harder model, and is out of scope.


7. dXl/dt COMES FROM A MONOTONE FIT, NOT FROM A DIFFERENCE
----------------------------------------------------------
X:Lysed is the noisiest series in the dataset, and M3 needs its slope at
every timepoint rather than only at harvest. Differencing it repeatedly
would be hopeless, so a smooth monotone curve is fitted per run and the
derivative taken analytically.

FORM. Non-negative least squares on an integrated B-spline basis. The
derivative is expanded directly,

    Xl'(t) = sum_k c_k B_k(t),   c_k >= 0

and the curve is its integral. B-spline basis functions are non-negative
everywhere, so non-negative coefficients make the derivative non-negative
EVERYWHERE, not merely at the knots. Monotonicity is thus a property of
the construction rather than something imposed by clipping afterwards.

This is a physical constraint on a cumulative pool, not a smoothing
convenience. The alternatives all fail one requirement: PCHIP is monotone
but interpolates, so the noise passes straight into Xl'; isotonic
regression is monotone but piecewise constant, so its derivative is a
train of spikes; an unconstrained smoothing spline smooths but can turn
downwards. NNLS is convex with an exact finite solution, so there is no
starting guess and no local minimum.

SETTINGS. Degree-2 derivative basis, one interior knot every 5 days,
chosen by leave-one-point-out cross-validation pooled over all 120 runs
(LOO RMS 0.01257). The top three candidates lay within 2% of each other,
so cross-validation did not decide between them; the tie was broken on
smoothness, because a degree-2 basis is C1 and therefore gives Xd(t) no
kinks. Residual RMS is 0.0062 against a measured noise sd of about
0.0087, so the fit is not chasing noise.

Knot SPACING rather than knot COUNT is a choice: it gives a 7-day and a
14-day run the same flexibility per unit time, where a fixed count would
give short runs more.

FREE INTERCEPT. The curve is not forced through Xl(0) = 0. Noise on
X:Lysed is clipped at zero (assumption 8), which biases the observations
upward early and not late; forcing the curve through the origin would
make it tilt to absorb that bias and corrupt the derivative, which is the
quantity actually wanted. The intercept is harmless because every use of
Xl here is via a difference, in which a constant offset cancels. It is
empirically tiny in any case (median 0.00012).

CONSEQUENCE 1 -- kl SHIFTS. The fitted harvest slope runs about 45% above
the 4-point window this replaces (median 0.0333 against 0.0205), because
a straight line through the last four points of a convex accelerating
curve carries that window's AVERAGE slope and so understates the slope AT
harvest. The old estimator was biased low. Since the model only ever sees
Xl'(T)/kl and kl is refitted, the scale factor is absorbed by kl rather
than changing the fit: expect kl about 1.45x larger than previously
recorded, with alpha and beta reproducing.

This also removes the arbitrary window whose choice swung held-out RMSE
between 1430 and 1648. The fit uses every point and has no window to
select, so a known sensitivity disappears rather than being tuned away.

CONSEQUENCE 2 -- SEVEN RUNS GET Xd(T) = 0. Seven of 120 runs receive a
fitted harvest slope of exactly zero (four did under the old window). All
seven are SHORT TRAINING runs of 7-8 days; none is a test run. Four of
them have final X:Lysed of exactly 0.0000, so zero is not implausible but
correct -- a culture with no measured lysate offers no lysate-derived
evidence of a dead pool. The other three end at 0.0045-0.0132, inside the
bottom 9% of all runs and below the 0.0087 noise floor, with measured
tail slopes of 0.002-0.003 that are not distinguishable from noise. Any
non-zero estimate there would be fitted noise. The dead-pool term
contributes a median 2-6% of cells synthesised across the dataset, and
would contribute far less than that for these runs, so the effect on the
fit is negligible. Recorded rather than patched.


8. MEASUREMENT NOISE ON X:Lysed IS ADDITIVE AND CLIPPED AT ZERO
---------------------------------------------------------------
Earlier notes described X:Lysed as carrying "roughly 7% noise". Measured
on the data, that is not the noise model:

  - It is ADDITIVE with a roughly constant absolute sd of about 0.0087,
    not multiplicative. The correlation between an early-window noise
    estimate and a run's final X:Lysed is +0.003, i.e. none.

  - It is CLIPPED AT ZERO. 48% of early values are exactly 0.0 and the
    minimum across all 1290 rows is exactly 0, which is what symmetric
    additive noise truncated at a floor looks like.

"7%" is that fixed absolute sd divided by the MEDIAN run's final X:Lysed
of 0.072. It is a ratio of scales, not a proportional noise model.

This matters beyond pedantry. Signal-to-noise at harvest is about 8 for a
median run but about 2 for a short run ending near X:Lysed = 0.02, so
SIGNAL QUALITY IS CORRELATED WITH RUN DURATION -- the exact axis of the
train/test shift. Expect Xd to be least determined in precisely the short
runs, which is where the seven zero-slope runs of assumption 7 sit.


9. Xl LEVELS COME FROM THE FITTED CURVE EVERYWHERE
--------------------------------------------------
C(t) = Xv(t) + Xl_fit(t) + Xl_fit'(t)/kl, at interior timepoints and at
the endpoints alike, so there is one definition of C rather than two.

Required for the identity sum_j dC_j = Delta(Xv+Xd+Xl) to hold exactly,
which is what makes M1, M2 and M3 genuinely nested rather than
approximately so. Forced independently by M3: 247 of the 1170 measured
X:Lysed steps decrease, so raw levels would make dC_j negative in roughly
21% of intervals -- the very pathology the monotone fit exists to remove.

Consequence: fitted Xl(T) differs from the measured value by a median
4.4%, a small further shift in M1 relative to the previously recorded
benchmark, on top of the kl shift in assumption 7.


10. SCREENING FEATURES -- CHOICES THAT WERE NOT FORCED
------------------------------------------------------
These affect stage-1 variable screening only. The models fit directly
against measured titre, so none of them touches the rate law.

  - MISSING VALUES ARE REPORTED AS nan, NOT IMPUTED. A late-phase
    exposure is undefined for a run that ended at the split day (30 of
    120 runs), and VCD at a process shift is undefined for a run that
    never reached it (56 of 120 for tempShift, 59 for phShift, 80 for
    FeedEnd). In both cases the missingness is perfectly collinear with
    duration, so filling it would inject a duration signal disguised as a
    metabolite or shift effect -- the exact confounding the fixed
    absolute phase split exists to avoid. Screening must therefore adopt
    an explicit policy rather than inherit a hidden one.

  - THE PHASE SPLIT IS AT A FIXED ABSOLUTE DAY (7), not at each run's own
    midpoint, which would make "late" mean days 3.5-7 in a short run and
    7-14 in a long one -- different regimes, confounded with duration.

  - SPECIFIC GROWTH RATE USES THE INTERVAL-MEAN VCD as its denominator.
    "dX_j/(X_j dt_j)" is ambiguous about which X_j; the interval mean is
    the same quantity the cell-day trapezoid uses and is better
    conditioned than the left endpoint, which is tiny in the first
    interval.

  - THE FEED WINDOW IS THE ONE REALISED BEFORE HARVEST, clipped to the
    run, rather than the designed FeedEnd - FeedStart. A feed window
    ending after harvest was never fully applied.


11. REFERENCE VALUES AND SEARCH RANGES
--------------------------------------
Reference points for the two exponential factors are the mid-range of the
observed data: 36.5 degC (observed 35.0-38.0) and pH 6.75 (observed
6.03-7.50). A reference sets only the scale of beta, which absorbs it;
it exists so beta reads as productivity at a nominal set point rather
than at 0 degC or pH 0.

Search ranges for the saturation and inhibition constants deliberately
reach an order of magnitude beyond the observed concentrations, so that
"this mechanism does nothing" (a very large K) lies inside the grid. The
fit must be able to REJECT a mechanism rather than be obliged to find an
effect for it.

The Monod and inhibition factors are bounded in (0, 1] and monotone, so
outside the fitted range they flatten rather than diverge -- which is why
they are preferred to linear or polynomial terms given that test runs
reach X:Lysed 1.02 against a training maximum of 0.53, and glucose 56.8
against 44.0. The exponential temperature and pH factors are NOT bounded;
that is acceptable only because both variables are controlled and the
test set stays inside the training range (temperature 35.05-37.95 against
35.09-37.99, pH 6.03-7.47 against 6.03-7.50). There is no extrapolation
on those two axes for the exponential to run away on.
